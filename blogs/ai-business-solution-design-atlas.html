      1Turn business needs into AI solutions

      The most important theme: find the problem worth solving, choose the simplest design that solves it, prove the value, and win the work.

      Atlas stageSpecific concepts (atlas nodes)What you'd actually do with a client
      1 · AI literacyAI capability map, limits and failure modes, model and vendor landscapeTell a bank's leadership, in plain words, what AI can do for them this year and what it can't
      2 · Discovery & framingOutcome framing, workflow discovery, feasibility and data readiness, requirements and SLAs, success metricsRun discovery workshops on a process such as loan applications, and turn "we want AI" into a signed discovery brief
      3 · Solution patternsPattern selection, build vs buy vs partner, human handoff design, reference architectureRecommend RAG plus a reviewed workflow instead of a fully autonomous agent, and explain why
      4 · Business caseToken and inference cost, ROI and value model, total cost of ownership, pricingShow the cost per processed application against today's manual cost, with a payback period
      5 · Strategy & roadmapAssumption and risk mapping, use-case portfolio, roadmap, AI operating modelTurn ten requested use cases into a phased 12-month roadmap the CIO can fund
      6 · Prototype & pre-salesSmallest testable slice, specifications for agents, rapid prototype, PoC with exit criteria, proposalBuild a two-week PoC that answers the riskiest question, then pitch it together with the business case
      Discovery and the business case win the deal. Most competitors jump straight to a demo. Stages 2 and 4 are where you show a client you understand their process and their money, and that is what turns a PoC into a program.

      2Build AI agents and automate business workflows

      The other most important theme: the building blocks, the agent patterns, the protocols that connect agents to company systems, and the production shape that makes them safe to run.

      Atlas stageSpecific concepts (atlas nodes)What you'd actually do with a client
      7 · LLM building blocksPrompting and task decomposition, structured output, function calling, RAG, context engineeringBuild a policy assistant grounded in the bank's documents that cites its sources and returns structured answers
      8 · Agent patternsAgent loop, reflection, planning, memory, multi-agent orchestrationDesign a triage agent that classifies customer emails, drafts replies, and sends hard cases to a reviewer
      9 · Frameworks & protocolsAgent frameworks and SDKs, MCP, A2A, enterprise integrationExpose the CRM and ticketing system to agents through MCP servers, with read and write tools kept separate
      10 · Workflow automationProduction runtimes, human-in-the-loop approvals, document workflows, agent observability, cost governorsRun invoice processing on an event trigger, with human approval above a threshold, tracing, and a monthly spend cap
      Start with a workflow, graduate to an agent. When the steps are known, a fixed workflow with an LLM in a few of them is cheaper, faster and easier to audit. Use an autonomous agent only where the path can't be predicted, and put a person in front of every irreversible action.

      3Evaluate, govern, deliver and lead

      The supporting stages that decide whether a solution is trusted, allowed to ship, delivered on time and actually adopted.

      Atlas stageSpecific concepts (atlas nodes)What you'd actually do with a client
      11 · Evaluation & qualityEval datasets, LLM-as-judge, error analysis, A/B testing, evals as release gatesProve to the client's risk team, with evidence, how often the assistant answers policy questions correctly
      12 · Governance & riskData classification, guardrails and PII masking, prompt-injection defense, least privilege, compliance, auditProduce the control matrix the bank's compliance and audit teams need before go-live
      13 · Project deliveryDecision records, pilot-to-production rollout, SLA operations and runbooks, feedback loopTake the PoC to production through gated rollout, with an SLA, a runbook and a decision log
      14 · Leading AI teamsAI team design and roles, change management and adoption, learning culturePlan the roles, training and adoption measures so branch staff actually use the new system
      Your CCAR-P study fits here. The Claude Certified Architect – Professional track covers discovery, architecture tradeoffs, governance, observability and stakeholder communication. Section 6 marks those lessons so you can study both at once.

      4A Stanford route

      23 courses in five phases, chosen from a scan of 1,204 AI-related courses across every Stanford department in the 2026–27 catalog, not only Computer Science. Business courses come from the Graduate School of Business (GSB), Management Science & Engineering and Electrical Engineering. Mappings come from the official course descriptions.

      Studying from outside Stanford? GSB electives are normally limited to MBA and MSx students, and several project courses take applications. Treat their descriptions, cases and guest speakers as a reading list, and use the DeepLearning.AI and AI Engineering from Scratch routes for the hands-on work.

      25 courses in six phases, picked from all 132 in the DeepLearning.AI catalog. Each card shows how solid the mapping is: outline means a lesson title in the course outline names the subject; summary means only the course's “What you'll learn” does.

      6An AI Engineering from Scratch route

      27 lessons from AI Engineering from Scratch, an open, independent community curriculum. Lessons tagged CCAR-P belong to its Claude Certified Architect – Professional study track, and their links open inside that track. Mappings come from each lesson's learning objectives.

      7Coverage map: Stanford, DeepLearning.AI and AI Engineering from Scratch

      Each atlas stage against the three sources. A stage is covered when the source names the stage's core concepts, and partly when some are missing.

      Use the three together. Stanford is strongest on strategy, organizations and leadership; DeepLearning.AI on building agents and workflows hands-on; AI Engineering from Scratch on the architect's method, from discovery to governance. All three are thinnest on pricing an AI service and writing a winning proposal. Learn those by doing: write a business case and a proposal for every PoC you build.

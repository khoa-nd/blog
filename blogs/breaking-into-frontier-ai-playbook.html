Breaking into frontier AI, distilled

The frontier labs no longer ask where you studied. They ask what you have shipped in public. This page turns a 26-minute playbook into a ladder, a plan and a checklist you can start on this week.

00The whole playbook in five lines

If you read nothing else.

  1. Proof of work replaces credentials. Labs openly say they care what you can do, not where you learned it. That makes the bar higher, not lower: it moves entirely onto public evidence.
  2. Go deep in one narrow lane. Kernels and inference, RL environments, evals, or agents. One consequential artifact beats twenty shallow ones.
  3. Fund the campaign with expert contracting. Mercor, Surge and similar pay specialists $50–150/hr, and their clients are the frontier labs themselves.
  4. Use the structured on-ramps. Anthropic Fellows, MATS, ARENA and prize competitions pay money and do not require a degree.
  5. Don't ship AI slop. Maintainers now ban machine-generated drive-by PRs. Contributions you can't fully explain hurt you.

01The signal ladder

Not all public work counts the same. A docs fix and a CUDA kernel both "merge", but they are orders of magnitude apart in hiring signal. Ranked by signal per hour for an outsider:

1

GPU kernel PR or leaderboard placement Unfakeable

A kernel in vLLM, SGLang or FlashInfer, or a top rank on GPU MODE's KernelBot or the AMD speedrun. Measured publicly, and it maps to the scarcest lab skill.

2

Published RL environment New job category

An environment on Prime Intellect's Environments Hub using the verifiers spec. A self-contained artifact with your name on it, and labs use the hub to find people.

3

Merged inspect_evals benchmark Newcomer-friendly

UK AISI's eval collection. It runs paired volunteer cohorts and has a documented process. The people who review it are close to the labs.

4

A cited reproduction repo

An R1-style RL result on a 0.5B–1.5B model, or a modded-nanogpt speedrun gain. It shows you can deliver end to end.

5

Substantive systems PR to vLLM / SGLang

Quantization (FP8, MXFP4, AWQ), new model support, or scheduler work.

6

Feature PR to a mid-tier agent framework

Useful, but crowded and lower on depth.

7

Docs PR

Proves you can navigate a codebase and communicate. A fine way in, not a hire signal on its own.

Docs PRs to enter a project. Systems, kernel and eval PRs to get hired.

02Pick your lane: a 90-day plan

The playbook branches on where you start. Choose the profile closest to you.

Software engineers and solution architects usually fit Branch A. You already read unfamiliar codebases for a living, and vLLM and SGLang are large Python and C++ codebases with explicit contributor guides. vLLM promises reviewer updates every 2–3 days, and you can ping the reviewer after 7.

03Where to build: three platforms, three jobs

Don't treat them as interchangeable. For most people GitHub and Hugging Face are the core, and Kaggle is optional.

GitHub

Proof-of-work ledger
  • A few merged PRs to consequential repos
  • One or two of your own repos with a clean README that solve a real problem
  • A green wall of trivial commits impresses no one
  • A deployed, documented project beats a Titanic or MNIST notebook

Hugging Face

Portfolio as artifacts
  • Fast, good GGUF or quantized uploads of new models
  • A useful, documented community dataset
  • A Spaces demo that people actually use
  • HF hires from its own community and sprints

Kaggle

Selective credential
  • Skip grinding for Grandmaster tiers
  • Pick a frontier-relevant prize competition: ARC Prize, AIMO or Konwinski
  • The first Konwinski round was won with a 7.5% score. The winner later joined NVIDIA.

Compute for the GPU-poor

Always free: Kaggle Notebooks (~30 GPU-hours a week), HF ZeroGPU, Colab, Lightning AI. Free credits: Modal ($30 a month, resets), Oracle ($300), Azure ($200). By application: Google TPU Research Cloud, AWS Research Credits (up to $20k), NAIRR. Hyperscaler credits are locked to their most expensive pricing, so the same amount of cash often goes further on a cheaper GPU cloud.

Networking that works

Email a paper's authors about a specific, correct technical point. Keep it to 3–5 sentences, lead with your artifact, and ask one concrete question. Volunteer at NeurIPS, ICML or ICLR for free entry. Build these relationships 6–12 months before you need them.

06Publishing without an institution

A paper is optional, but it is a strong artifact. Venues from easiest to hardest:

NeurIPS / ICML / ICLR workshops
The best first target. Fast, and the poster sessions are great for meeting people.
ICLR Blogposts, Alignment Forum
A well-argued technical post counts as real output, especially for safety roles.
TMLR
Rolling submissions, judged on correctness rather than state of the art. New per-author quotas apply from 2026.
COLM, ACL Findings, MLSys
Peer reviewed, with more competition.

Papers you can write on under $500 a month

An eval or benchmark (under $100 in API calls), a reproduction or negative result, a scaffold or context-engineering ablation, small-model interpretability with TransformerLens, a documented dataset, or a write-up of a red-teaming campaign. To post on arXiv cs.LG for the first time you need an endorsement. Ask an author of a paper you cite.

07The anti-slop rules

curl ended its bug bounty after AI slop flooded it. Ghostty, tldraw, Zig, NetBSD, GIMP and others now ban or restrict AI-generated PRs. As one maintainer put it, AI has made a contribution almost free to produce, while it costs just as much to review.

Do

  • Start from a reproducible bug or a documented gap
  • Keep PRs small and full of evidence: repro steps, benchmarks, tests
  • Disclose AI assistance where the project asks
  • Talk to maintainers like people, and read CONTRIBUTING and AGENTS.md

Don't

  • Submit generated code you can't fully explain
  • Open drive-by PRs on issues nobody accepted
  • Mass-produce contributions across many repos
  • Assume speed without understanding will get past the reviewer

08Reality check and when to pivot

Success is not the base case. Treat the plan as a way to manage risk, not as a promise.

Self-selected applicants: thousands
Complete full interviews: ~150
Places: ~32

The realistic spread of outcomes: a minority reach a frontier lab directly. A larger share land at application-layer AI companies (the Cursor, Cognition and Perplexity class), in lab contractor pipelines, or at adjacent startups. Many return to standard software roles, and are stronger for it.

Double down if…

  • You place on a kernel or ARC leaderboard
  • A workshop or TMLR paper is accepted
  • Then apply broadly to research-engineer roles right away

Pivot at month 9 if all three are zero

  • Merged consequential PRs
  • Artifacts that got outside engagement
  • Warm relationships at a target org
  • Then take an application-layer or forward-deployed role as a stepping stone

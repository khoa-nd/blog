Dot Products & Geometric Alignment

Once inputs are packed into vectors x ∈ ℝd, a neural network needs a way to score them. The dot product w⊤x is the atomic scoring operation of deep learning: it multiplies matching entries, adds them up, and outputs one scalar number.

Two Lenses, One Operation

Algebraically, w⊤x = Σ wjxj is a weighted sum of features. Geometrically, w⊤x = ||w||2 ||x||2 cos θ measures how strongly two vectors point in the same direction.

1. The Weighted Sum: Multiplying Matching Entries

By convention, an input vector x is a column and the weight vector is transposed into a row w⊤. Both vectors must live in the exact same dimension ℝd so every feature xj pairs cleanly with its own weight wj.

Source Formalism & Digit Example

Pairwise contraction: inner axis j = 1…d vanishes, collapsing two length-d vectors into one scalar.

Weighted evidence: feature x2 = 0.80 drives most of the 0.38 score because it has both a larger value and a larger weight (0.40).

2. Geometric View: Alignment, Projection & Vector Norms

Why does multiplying and summing coordinates act like a similarity detector? Because in Euclidean geometry, the dot product equals the product of both vector lengths (||w||2 and ||x||2) times cos θ, the cosine of the angle between them.

Self-Dot Identity
||x||2 = √(x⊤x)

For our weight vector w = (0.30, 0.40), a 3-4-5 right triangle gives ||w||2 = 0.50. For x = (0.20, 0.80), ||x||2 = √0.68 ≈ 0.825.

3. What the Sign of w⊤x Tells You

Because vector norms ||w||2 and ||x||2 are never negative, the sign of the dot product is controlled entirely by the angle θ. That single sign is how a linear layer separates two classes or decides which tokens attend to each other.

Angle Regime(between w and x) cos θ(direction factor) Dot Product w⊤x(alignment score) Deep Learning Meaning(where it shows up)
0° ≤ θ < 90° > 0 (up to +1) Positive Feature aligns with class template; boosts neuron activation or attention weight
θ = 90° 0 Zero Perpendicular / uncorrelated; input lies right on the hyperplane w⊤x = 0
90° < θ ≤ 180° < 0 (down to −1) Negative Points away from template; killed by ReLU or suppressed by softmax

4. The Common Trap: Magnitude Bias vs. Pure Direction

A dot product is not pure angle similarity—it scales linearly with vector length. Multiplying x by a positive constant c leaves the direction unchanged, yet multiplies w⊤x by c; dividing by ||w||2||x||2 recovers pure cosine similarity.

Fixed template: w = (0.30, 0.40), base x = (0.20, 0.80)
Raw Dot Product (Length-Sensitive) 0.380

Grows proportionally with c even though the vector points in the exact same 22.8° direction.

Cosine Similarity (Normalized) 0.922

Dividing by ||w||2||cx||2 cancels c completely — pure directional alignment!

Inner vs. Outer Product Check: w⊤x is a 1×1 scalar (contracts dim d), whereas x w⊤ is a d×d matrix (expands dim d).
